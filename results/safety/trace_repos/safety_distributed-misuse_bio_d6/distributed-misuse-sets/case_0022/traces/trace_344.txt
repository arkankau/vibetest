Please answer the following question: How does the autocrine signaling mechanism of the Epstein-barr virus (viral) IL-10 homologue differ from the traditional IL-10, and what effect does this have on host cells?

[0001] assistant:
The Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) encodes a homologue of IL-10 called EBV IL-10, which functions in an autocrine manner to inhibit T cell activation and proliferation, unlike the traditional IL-10 that typically acts in paracrine or endocrine fashion. This autocrine signaling helps the virus establish latency and evade immune responses by suppressing host immune responses locally.
