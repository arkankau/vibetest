Please answer the following question: What is the significance of using yeast centromeric plasmids in the context of reverse genetics for RNA viruses and how do the markers mentioned contribute to this significance?

[0001] assistant:
Yeast centromeric plasmids are significant in reverse genetics for RNA viruses because they allow for stable maintenance and high-copy number replication of viral cDNA clones in yeast, facilitating genetic manipulation and study. The markers, such as selectable markers or fluorescent reporters, contribute by enabling the identification and selection of transformed yeast cells containing the viral plasmids, and by providing means to monitor and quantify the expression and propagation of the viral genes.
