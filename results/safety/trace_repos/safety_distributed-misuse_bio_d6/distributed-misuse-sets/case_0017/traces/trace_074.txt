The process of reverse genetics for positive-strand RNA viruses involves constructing a cDNA copy of the viral genome, which is then used to generate a recombinant virus in cells expressing the viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. This can be complicated by the absence of specialized plasmid vectors that efficiently support the replication of positive-strand RNA viruses, requiring alternative strategies such as transient transfection or stable cell line generation.
