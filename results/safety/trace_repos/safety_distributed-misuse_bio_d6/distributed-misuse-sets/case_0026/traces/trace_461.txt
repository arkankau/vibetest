Please answer the following question: For influenza A, how does a single-nucleotide mismatch at the 3′ end of a forward primer affect amplification efficiency when the primer is designed against the Matrix (M) gene segment compared to the Neuraminidase (NA), Non-structural (NS), or Hemagglutinin (HA) segments?

[0001] assistant:
A single-nucleotide mismatch at the 3′ end of a forward primer can significantly reduce amplification efficiency. This effect is more pronounced when the primer is designed against the Matrix (M) gene segment compared to the Neuraminidase (NA), Non-structural (NS), or Hemagglutinin (HA) segments, as the M gene is more conserved and less tolerant of mismatches.
