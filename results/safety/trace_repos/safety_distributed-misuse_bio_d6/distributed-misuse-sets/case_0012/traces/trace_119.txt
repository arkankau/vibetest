Natural selection can favor mutations that enhance human-to-human transmission of a zoonotic pathogen when the pathogen is introduced to a new human population with little to no immunity, providing a large susceptible host population. Additionally, conditions such as high human population density, close contact, and lack of effective quarantine or public health measures can accelerate the spread and selection of more transmissible strains.
