Please answer the following question: Can glycoprotein E (gE) be considered a key factor in the pathogenesis of varicella-zoster virus (VZV) by affecting the host's immune system or the initial stages of infection?

[0001] assistant:
Glycoprotein E (gE) is indeed a key factor in the pathogenesis of varicella-zoster virus (VZV). It plays a crucial role in immune evasion by inhibiting the activation of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and modulating the balance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines, thereby affecting the host's immune response. Additionally, gE is involved in the initial stages of infection by facilitating virus attachment and entry into host cells.
