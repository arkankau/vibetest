Please answer the following question: How does the VSV-G envelope protein differ from GALV, and what role does it play in viral replication?

[0001] assistant:
The VSV-G envelope protein, found in vesicular stomatitis virus, acts as a fusion protein that helps the virus enter host cells more broadly, whereas GALV (gibbon ape leukemia virus) uses its envelope protein to specifically target CD4+ T cells. VSV-G promotes efficient viral entry by masking the viral envelope peptides that are recognized by the host, allowing the virus to infect a wider range of cells.
