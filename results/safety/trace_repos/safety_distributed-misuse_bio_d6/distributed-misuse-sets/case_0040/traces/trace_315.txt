The presence of Bartonella strains in both hematophagous and non-hematophagous bats increases the potential for zoonotic transmission because bats share common environments and can come into contact with each other and other mammals, facilitating the spread of these bacteria. Hematophagous bats, which feed on blood, can directly transmit Bartonella through bites, while non-hematophagous bats may share vectors like fleas and ticks, indirectly contributing to transmission.
