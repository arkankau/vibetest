Please answer the following question: What are the toxic effects of feeding atropa belladonna to food animals, and are these effects transferable to humans through consumption of the meat or milk from these animals?

[0001] assistant:
Atropa belladonna, also known as deadly nightshade, contains toxic alkaloids such as atropine, hyoscyamine, and scopolamine. Feeding it to food animals can cause poisoning in the animals, leading to symptoms like dilated pupils, fever, tachycardia, and in severe cases, paralysis and respiratory failure. These toxic effects are not typically transferable to humans through the consumption of meat or milk from affected animals, as the toxic alkaloids are not bioaccumulated in significant amounts in animal tissues.
