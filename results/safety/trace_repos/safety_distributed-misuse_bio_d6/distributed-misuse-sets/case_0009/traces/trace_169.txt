NAD (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) plays a crucial role in cellular energy metabolism by serving as a coenzyme in redox reactions, facilitating the transfer of electrons and thereby supporting various metabolic pathways like glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation. NAD is not a substrate for diphtheria toxin; instead, diphtheria toxin inactivates NAD-dependent enzymes by ADP-ribosylation, specifically targeting elongation factor EF-2.
