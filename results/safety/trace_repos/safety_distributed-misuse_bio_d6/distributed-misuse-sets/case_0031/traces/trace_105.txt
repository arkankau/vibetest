Viral envelope proteins, such as hemagglutinin and neuraminidase, play a crucial role in the transmissibility of pandemic influenza viruses by mediating viral attachment to host cells and facilitating infection. When modified during the reconstruction process using reverse genetics, these proteins can alter the virus's ability to infect and spread between hosts, potentially affecting the virus's transmissibility.
