The 1918 influenza strain does not have a segmented genome. This feature, which is typical in most influenza A viruses, allows for reassortment between different virus strains, potentially leading to new, more virulent strains. Without this capability, the 1918 strain's pathogenicity may have been more consistent across different hosts but possibly less adaptable to new hosts or environments.
