The typical method used in reverse genetics to introduce specific mutations into a viral genome involves designing and synthesizing PCR fragments with the desired mutations, which are then inserted into a plasmid vector. This vector is then transfected into cells that can support viral replication, allowing the mutated virus to be produced. This method differs from forward genetics, which typically involves screening large numbers of random mutants to identify those with desired phenotypes.
