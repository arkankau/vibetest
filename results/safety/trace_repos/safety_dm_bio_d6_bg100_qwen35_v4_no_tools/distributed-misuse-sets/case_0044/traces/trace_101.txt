Increased mutation rates can lead to rapid adaptation, allowing zoonotic pathogens to better survive outside the host and potentially infect humans more effectively. Survival outside the host longer enhances the pathogen's opportunity to find a new host, including humans. Decreased virulence might initially seem counterintuitive but can facilitate transmission by allowing the pathogen to spread more widely before causing severe disease, thus increasing the likelihood of exposure to new hosts.
