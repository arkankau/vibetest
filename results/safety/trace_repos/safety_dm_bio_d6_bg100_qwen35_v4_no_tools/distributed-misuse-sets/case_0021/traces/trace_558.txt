Please answer the following question: How common are Fungi as hosts for human infectious agents, and what specific characteristics of Fungi contribute to this?

[0001] assistant:
Fungi are relatively uncommon as direct hosts for human infectious agents compared to bacteria and viruses. However, certain fungi, like Cryptococcus neoformans and Histoplasma capsulatum, can cause significant infections in humans, often in immunocompromised individuals. Characteristics like their ability to form resistant structures (e.g., spores), adapt to different environmental conditions, and evade host immune responses contribute to their potential as pathogens.
