Please answer the following question: In a CRISPR homing gene drive that cuts a single genomic site in mosquitoes, how do non-homologous end-joining (NHEJ) repair events give rise to resistance alleles, and what molecular features of the target locus make such resistant mutations more or less likely to prevent subsequent Cas9 cutting?

[0001] assistant:
Non-homologous end-joining (NHEJ) repair often results in small insertions or deletions (indels) that can disrupt the Cas9 target site, thereby preventing subsequent Cas9 cutting. The presence of short, repetitive sequences or flexible regions in the target locus can increase the likelihood of indel formation and resistance.
