Please answer the following question: What specific mechanisms do hydrophobic molecules use to penetrate the bacterial outer membrane and why is this significant in the context of antimicrobial resistance?

[0001] assistant:
Hydrophobic molecules can penetrate the bacterial outer membrane through porins or by disrupting the membrane's lipid bilayer due to their non-polar nature. This is significant in antimicrobial resistance because it allows certain compounds to bypass the membrane's barrier, potentially increasing the effectiveness of certain drugs or natural compounds against bacteria that have developed resistance.
