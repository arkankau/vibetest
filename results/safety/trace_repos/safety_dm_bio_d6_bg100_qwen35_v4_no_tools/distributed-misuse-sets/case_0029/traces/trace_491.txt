The polarity of the RNA genome influences the type of host cell used in reverse genetics systems because viruses with different genome polarities (positive-sense or negative-sense) require host cells with compatible translation mechanisms. Positive-sense RNA viruses can directly translate their genome, while negative-sense RNA viruses require an intermediate step of transcription by viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. Therefore, the choice of host cell must match the virus's polarity to support its replication and expression.
