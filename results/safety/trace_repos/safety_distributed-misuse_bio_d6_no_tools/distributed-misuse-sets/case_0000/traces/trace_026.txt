DNA-launched infectious systems offer an advantage over RNA-launched systems in terms of antigenic variability for vaccine development because DNA can be stably integrated into the host genome, potentially leading to longer-lasting expression of the antigen and fewer generations of replication, which may reduce the risk of mutations that could alter the antigenic properties.
