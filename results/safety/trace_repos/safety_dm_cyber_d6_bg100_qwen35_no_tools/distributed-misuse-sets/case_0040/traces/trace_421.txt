Please answer the following question: In a standard Modbus TCP/IP session, what purpose does the 2-byte transaction identifier serve, and how do commercial IDS engines use it to spot replayed commands?

[0001] assistant:
The 2-byte transaction identifier in a Modbus TCP/IP session uniquely identifies a request and its corresponding response. Commercial IDS engines use this identifier to detect and block replayed commands by recognizing when the same identifier is reused for a new transaction, indicating a potential replay attack.
