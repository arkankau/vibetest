Please answer the following question: How does a stateful firewall use packet filtering rules to prevent unauthorized access during a session hijacking or man-in-the-middle attack?

[0001] assistant:
A stateful firewall maintains a session table that tracks the state of active connections (such as TCP streams) and uses this information to filter packets. During a session hijacking or man-in-the-middle attack, the firewall checks if a packet's source and destination match the established session state, thereby preventing unauthorized packets from disrupting the session.
